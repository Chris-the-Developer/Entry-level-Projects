    Punctuation guide

    Punctuation changes how a sentence sounds when you read it aloud. Press Hear to listen.

    .

    Period (full stop)

    Ends a statement.

    I live in Lisbon.

    Your voice goes down, then you pause.

    ,

    Comma

    Separates items in a list, and opening words like “However”.

    However, I was late.

    A short pause.

    ?

    Question mark

    Ends a direct question.

    Do you live here?

    Yes/no questions usually go up at the end. Questions with what, where or why usually go down.

    !

    Exclamation mark

    Shows strong feeling. One is enough.

    That's amazing!

    Say it with more energy.

    '

    Apostrophe

    Shows missing letters (don't = do not) or that something belongs to someone (Anna's bag). Never use it for plurals: apples, not apple's.

    Don't touch Anna's bag.

    You don't hear it, so check it when you write.

    :

    Colon

    Introduces a list or an explanation.

    You need three things: a pen, paper and time.

    A pause that says “here it comes”.

    ;

    Semicolon

    Joins two complete sentences that are closely connected.

    It was late; we went home.

    A pause, longer than a comma.

    “ ”

    Quotation marks

    Show the exact words someone said.

    She said, “I'm tired.”

    Change your voice a little for the quote.
